# Style system

## Fixed identity

- Use a warm white, pale cream, or near-white background. Draw a compact vignette inside 20–45% of the canvas; leave 55–80% calm negative space.
- Draw with soft black, graphite, deep charcoal, or slightly dry black crayon. Establish a clear hierarchy: a broad, tactile, imperfect outer contour; thinner short marks only for faces, fingers, seams, or motion; and occasional solid black shape for a shoe, hair, hat, or shadow. Every stroke should follow a natural pressure arc: begin slightly light or tapered, press through the curve/weight-bearing section, then ease out into a rounded or lifted taper. Keep contours selectively open: let a line end before it completes a shape when overlap, direction, or a solid patch already explains the form. The outer contour should vary visibly in width (roughly 2–3:1) and have a softly grainy, hand-pressed edge—not a razor-clean vector edge. Never use one uniform line width, blunt cut-off endpoints, or fully closed outlines on every form.
- Make the image a tiny visual joke, not an illustration of a whole world. Use 1–2 simplified figures/objects, one clearly readable action, and at most two tiny supporting marks (ground line, star, motion dash, cloud, box, cup, etc.).
- Reduce people, animals, objects, and places to warm, awkward, recognisable silhouettes. Faces need only dots, a tiny nose, a mouth, or no face at all. Let a hand gesture, a slightly off-balance pose, or an unexpected interaction make it delightful.
- Use flat fills and very restrained texture: at most one small dry scribble, sparse stipple patch, or black silhouette patch. Do not use hatching. Do not render light, volume, architectural perspective, scenery, or full-frame paper texture.
- Use color only as a quiet anchor: none by default, one muted accent normally, two related accents only if necessary. Keep all color under 12% of the frame.

## Palette recipes

Start with ink `#25211F` and one pale ground. Choose one recipe unless the brief supplies approved brand colors.

| Recipe | Ground | Accent(s) | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ink and cream | `#F7F2E9` | none or muted peach `#ECA08D` | intimate, editorial, character-led |
| Dusty blue | `#EDF4F5` | slate blue `#7087C9`, faded coral `#E89B8D` | ideas, digital tools, optimism |
| Soft warm | `#F6F0E8` | ochre `#D9B35A`, clay `#C98370` | objects, craft, motion |
| Quiet green | `#F0F2EA` | sage `#9BAA91`, peach `#E3A08B` | nature, care, calm |

Never use pure RGB primaries, neon, gradients, glossy effects, or more than two accent hues. Desaturate any brand color before use unless brand accuracy overrides the system.

## Composition recipes

- **Tiny ground-line:** Put one or two subjects on a short wobbly baseline in the lower third; leave most of the page empty.
- **Floating encounter:** Make two simple forms meet or exchange something in the center; add only one orbiting mark or tiny star.
- **Small object joke:** Draw one familiar object performing an impossible but immediately legible action; use 1–3 motion marks.
- **Off-balance stack:** Balance 2–3 simple forms in an intentionally unstable, playful way; vary their spacing and tilt; no background scene.
- **Word companion:** Place a short handwritten phrase beside, not over, a small vignette; only when the user requests text.

## Scale rules

- Default subject footprint: 20–45% of the frame. A large canvas means more breathing room, not a bigger scene.
- Draw a requested vehicle, building, Ferris wheel, machine, or landscape as an icon-like small vignette unless the user explicitly asks for a detailed scene.
- Use 6 or fewer repeating elements by default. For potentially dense objects (Ferris wheel, city, crowd, shelves), show only the minimum marks needed for recognition.
- Break regularity through gesture, not distortion for its own sake: let contours overshoot a little, make one limb longer, tip the weight of a figure, overlap forms, or add one thick black patch. Avoid stiff frontal icons and centered diagram layouts. For a wheel, draw an interrupted, slightly misshapen hoop with only 3–4 loose inner marks; leave at least one opening in the hoop or at an inner-arc connection and never show a complete radial mechanism.
- Never add an elaborate skyline, multiple buildings, realistic trees, photographic ground, atmospheric sky, or perspective grid just to explain the subject.

## Variable controls

Vary the tiny joke, subject type, visual metaphor, compact placement, ground hue, one accent, texture note, and motion cue. Do not vary the system into poster layout, large scene, glossy illustration, children’s-cartoon density, photorealism, 3D, anime, technical drawing, or a direct imitation of a particular illustrator.

## Reference-analysis template

Treat the reference collection as a family of constraints, not one artist’s identity.
